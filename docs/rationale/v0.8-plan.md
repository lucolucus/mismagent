# 0.8 plan — builders in parallel, one integrator

The human asked (2026-11-06) where 0.7.27 goes next: specify writing cards beside the build today,
builders in parallel, each in a worktree, tomorrow — and whether git is the place for the cards at
all. Written down, the answer is: the cards stay where they are, on the integration branch, and
the integration checkout is the only place `.mismagent/slices/` is ever written. A builder reads
the slice it was given, the examples and the conventions at the commit it started from, and
writes code; whatever it has to say about the slice (progress, a question) goes to the
integration checkout through `mm`, not onto its branch. What a builder lacks is not a view of the
cards but a tree of its own, and git has one for that: a worktree on a branch. Hypothesis K7
(slices in sequence, one integrator) keeps its second half and loses its first: slices build in
parallel on branches; one conductor, in one checkout, moves every state and lands every slice, in
sequence.

Rejected on the way, on their costs: a branch of its own for `.mismagent/` (no working tree,
compare-and-swap writes, a mirror materialized per worktree) and a store outside git (SQLite in
`.git/`, a daemon). Every reader of live state — the conductor, the human, a builder asking or
resuming, a reviewer — is on the same disk and reads the integration checkout by path; a second
branch would add cross-ref consistency and a materialization step for each read, a store would
lose history, diffs and clones and still not make the checkout's update atomic; the provenance
stays the hooks' job either way. What the design gives up instead: with the state on the
integration branch, a slice's branch is always behind at land (`Built:`, `Tip:` and the review
landed after it started), so every land rebases the builder's commits — their shas never survive,
and merges stay out (`code_heads`: a merge never; a merge commit among the builder's is refused).

## One rule: what a review and a gate certify
A review and a gate are **certificates over inputs**, recorded as hashes, never as commits:
- **`TREE:`** — the code tree: the commit's tree with `.mismagent/` taken out, the filtered
  entries of `git ls-tree -r --full-tree` (modes and gitlinks included; run at the root; a git
  error is never an equality), hashed. A rebase makes the reviewed sha unreachable; its hash
  stays on the card and in the review.
- **`INPUTS:`** — the slice's manifest, defined positively, parsed, hashed: the author's headers
  of its card (`Kind`, `Release`, `Examples`, `Design`, `After`, `Base`) and every section of it —
  `## Progress`, every `## Question` and `## Answer` wherever they sit — never mm's operational
  headers; the examples it names and those whose acceptance files its diff touches, as parsed
  rows; the artboards it names; the decisions its card, its examples or `ARCHITECTURE.md` cite,
  and any decision that names one of those; for a `Design:` slice, the shots on the worktree's
  disk (`Shots:`, hashed by `built`/`integrate`). For a release review: the release file, every
  example of its slices, their artboards, the cited decisions, their cards. The manifest's files
  are read from git objects at a named integration sha (`git show <sha>:<path>`), never from a
  moving HEAD or the working files: the worktree's `.mismagent/` is a snapshot at `Base:`, never
  read.

The suite and the lint stand for any commit with the gated `TREE:`; a review stands for any
commit with the reviewed `TREE:` and `INPUTS:`; `check` is cheap and always runs again. So a
certificate survives mm's own card commits, the `review: <stem>` commit, a `## Answer` on
*another* card, a spec commit about another slice, a decision for another slice, and the rebase
of its branch over them; it is redone after a rebase over a landed slice (the reviewer told which
files moved under it: it reads the diff on the whole tree it lands in, as today), after an answer
on its own card, or after a change to its own examples, artboards or decisions. **What the
reviewer read is what is stamped**: the reviewer's first command is `mm manifest <target>`, which
prints the integration's sha, the manifest's complete path list at that sha and its hash; the
reviewer reads those paths at that sha and nothing else (the shots on the worktree's disk are
not written while no builder is in flight, and `mm review` hashes them again); `mm review --sha
<tip> --manifest <hash>@<sha>` recomputes the manifest at `<sha>` — it must be `<hash>`: what was
read — and at HEAD: equal, the review is stamped with it; different, refused, the inputs that
moved named. Membership is part of the projection: a decision added that names the card, or
withdrawn, changes the hash at HEAD; a change made and undone during the review is irrelevant,
because the reviewer never read HEAD. Operational headers (`Gate:` written by the reviewer's own
gate) are outside the projection, so the review does not invalidate itself. A review file without `TREE:` (written before 0.8) certifies nothing. Not a patch-id: `git
patch-id --stable` ignores whitespace, and a clean rebase over a change in a callee leaves a diff
identical and a behaviour changed. The one rule replaces 0.7.27's `code_heads` (its
"transparent" commits are the special case where the code tree is unchanged). The price is
counted (below): a change to `README.md`, `.gitattributes` or a mode bit is a changed code tree
too.

## The shape
- **The integration checkout** is the one `/mismagent:build` runs in, on the integration branch
  (`branch:` under `## mismagent` in `CLAUDE.md`; default the branch checked out at the first
  parallel `start`). It holds the cards and their state, the reviews, the notes, the proposals, the
  release shots, the tag. Specify works here beside the conductor, as 0.7.27 made possible. `mm`
  run from a worktree **acts in the integration checkout**: it resolves the caller's paths
  (`--body`) first, then finds the checkout through `git worktree list` (the one on the integration
  branch; two, or none: refused), makes it `ROOT`, and takes the slice's worktree from the card's
  `Worktree:` header, verified against `git worktree list` every time (a directory deleted by
  hand: `git worktree prune`, then provisioning again from `mm/<stem>`, said by `next`), for the
  code it reads and the commands it runs there; run from a worktree that is no slice's (the
  human's `par/<stem>`) it refuses, naming the path. The lock, `publish`'s private index and the
  candidate refs live in the common git dir (`--git-common-dir`): one lock for every worktree.
  `publish` is 0.7.27's, unchanged, always in `ROOT`: a header written into the checkout's working
  copy, committed from there, tree, index and HEAD agreeing afterwards — never a file copied from
  a branch.
- **A worktree per slice being built**, provisioned in stages the card records, each resumable.
  With `parallel: N` (N > 1) under `## mismagent`, `mm start <slice>`: (1) under the lock, the
  preflights — `.gitattributes` clean, no doing slice without a `Worktree:` other than one
  provisioning (one started at `parallel: 1` finishes under today's rules first; `parallel` is
  lowered only when no doing slice has a `Worktree:`), `mm/<stem>` free as a branch and as a path
  *unless* it is this slice's own, left by a cut (a registered worktree on `mm/<stem>` is
  adopted; a missing branch is created at the card's doing commit; a branch with commits of its
  own and no `Worktree:` is nobody's cut: refused, named) — then todo → doing with `Base:` and
  `Branch: mm/<stem>`, the union rules `.gitattributes` lacks added (that path alone, through the
  private index), commit, and `git branch mm/<stem>` at that commit; (2) the lock released, `git
  worktree add ../<dir>.mm/<stem> mm/<stem>`, `Worktree:` published; (3) `setup:` from
  `CLAUDE.md` there (dependencies, a database of its own) — **idempotent by contract**, like
  `test:`: run again after a cut, it must succeed on what a first run left — `Setup: ok`
  published; failed: `mm park` with `Setup: failed: <reason>` on the todo card, which `start`
  refuses until the human removes the line, the worktree and the branch removed when removal is
  clean, else `Cleanup: pending` and a `FOR-HUMAN` line (what `setup:` left outside the worktree
  is the project's); (4) the first dispatch. `next` says `start <slice>` for a doing slice whose
  stages are incomplete before anything else. With `parallel: 1`, the default, every path is
  0.7.27's: no worktree, no branch, no dispatch record, the builder synchronous in the checkout.
- **A builder writes code on its branch, never a card.** Plain commits by path in its worktree: it
  is alone there. Its own commits are `<integration>..<tip>`; under `.mismagent/` they may hold
  *additions* to `design-notes.md` and `conventions-proposals.md` only — lines added, none
  removed (line sets compared, the missing-newline artifact normalized), the mode compared on
  its own, never a deletion or a rename: the two files merge by union, and union does not merge
  a modify/delete. Its slice file is the integration checkout's copy — the copy on the branch is
  a snapshot at `Base:`, never read, never written. `## Progress` goes through `mm note <slice>
  "<line>"` — at every green step, as the prompt asks: it is the heartbeat too —, a doubt through
  `mm ask <slice> --body <file>` (a new `## Question` after any earlier answer, then `RESULT:
  BLOCKED` as today), and the conductor's or the human's `## Answer` through `mm answer <slice>
  --body <file>`: three locked moves on the checkout's copy. Each writes a journal — the
  operation's id, the card's preimage hash, the whole new content — to a temporary name,
  validates it, renames it atomically to `<card>.mm-pending` (ignored, like the drafts), writes
  the card, publishes, removes the journal; the next lock entry heals a cut, in this order: a working card neither the preimage nor the
  result → `NEEDS-HUMAN`, two hands, first; card at the preimage → write and publish; card at the
  result → publish (which reconciles the index on that path even when HEAD already holds the
  result, unrelated staged content kept); then remove the journal. Staged or unstaged dirt on that copy from any other hand
  is a `wait` (`note` retries three times, then the builder returns with the line in its result
  and `built` still pending). Shots a slice renders stay on the worktree's disk (ignored, as
  today), read there by the reviewer, hashed into the manifest, re-rendered when the slice's code
  tree changes; the release's set is committed at `tag` as today.
- **`mm built` from the worktree**: refused while a rebase is open there, while a tracked file is
  modified there, or while a branch commit breaks the `.mismagent/` rule or is a merge (the commit
  and the route named). It runs the gate there — `check` for *this* slice: its card from `ROOT`,
  its examples' acceptance markers in the worktree's tests, no other slice's markers (another
  doing slice's are not there; the done slices' are checked where the tree is current, at
  `integrate` and `land`); `check --base` walks the builder's commits from `git merge-base
  <integration> <tip>` for the guarded files, `Base:` kept as the record of where it started —
  then the suite and the lint. It records `Built: <round>` (an integer, as the parser and the
  round arithmetic want), `Tip: <sha>`, `Tree:`, `Shots:` and `Gate:` on the integration copy,
  and closes its dispatch. **Built this round** means `Built:` is the round *and* `Tip:` is the
  branch's tip: a commit after `built` makes the slice `resume`, as today's "cut before `mm
  built`".
- **`mm integrate <slice>`**, no lock held, on a slice built this round and not in flight:
  records the integration tip on the card, rebases the branch onto it in the worktree
  (`GIT_EDITOR=true`); a dirty worktree refuses it, naming the path; a conflict leaves the rebase
  open and names the files — the action `integrate` dispatches the builder to resolve by hand and
  `GIT_EDITOR=true git rebase --continue` (`--skip` never: it drops a commit; `--abort` then `mm
  integrate --aborted <slice>`, which closes the dispatch as aborted: the slice is `escalate`, the
  architect decides how the two reconcile, and `stuck` after a DIRECT — never `integrate` again
  by itself); an open rebase with no unmerged file is continued by mm itself. The code tree
  changed under the slice: the gate runs again (`Gate:` written for it), with the done slices'
  markers now in the tree. `Tip:` and `Tree:` move to the new sha. Mid-rebase the worktree is
  detached: mm finds it by `Worktree:`, never by branch.
- **A review** is dispatched only on an integrated tip — the code tree of `git merge-base
  <integration> <tip>` is the integration's — and **synchronously**, as today: the reviewer and
  the architect take minutes, the tick waits for them, no dispatch record, nothing to close. The
  reviewer runs `mm manifest`, reads `<integration>...<tip>` in the worktree, runs the gate there
  as today, writes its draft, notes and proposals in the integration checkout; `mm review --sha
  <tip> --manifest …` refuses when the tip is no longer integrated or the manifest moved — the
  reviewer returns with the refusal, and the slice is `review` again next tick — and writes
  `SHA:`, `TREE:` and `INPUTS:`.
- **The human's own worktree** (`par/<stem>` by hand, read by the site) keeps its name space: `mm
  import <slice>` pins the human's tip and writes the ordered list `Importing: <sha> <sha> …`
  (the commits after the last `Imported:`, or after `git merge-base par/<stem> mm/<stem>` when the
  human amended); the builder takes **one commit at a time**: `cherry-pick -n <c>`, every
  `.mismagent/` path dropped (`git reset -- .mismagent`; a path HEAD has restored from HEAD, a
  path the pick introduced removed), a conflict resolved and staged, then a commit by path with
  the trailer `Imported-From: <c>` — or nothing to commit, for a commit that was all card — then
  `mm import --done <slice> <c>` (cut before it: the trailer on the branch is the outcome, never
  an "empty"), which records the source and its outcome (the
  builder's commit, or "empty") on the card under the lock; `git cherry-pick --quit` clears any
  state; `--skip` and `--continue` are not the route (`-n` leaves no sequencer); a card line in
  the human's commit is theirs to put on the card in the integration checkout. `built` refuses
  while a listed source has no outcome, and records `Imported: <pinned tip>` when every one has.

## Who is in flight
The card is the record of a dispatch, as it is of everything else. **Identity comes with every
call, not from a shared file**: the plugin's `PreToolUse(Bash)` hook rewrites a command that runs
`mm` through `updatedInput.command` to `export MM_SESSION=<session_id>; <command>` (the hook
input's `session_id`, documented; one export covers every `mm` in the command, `cd` and `;`
included), in the main session and in the agents' alike; a state move with no or a malformed
`MM_SESSION` is refused. The value is provenance, not authentication, and no proof of a death. **Before** each builder Agent call
the conductor runs `mm dispatch <slice> <action>`, which publishes `Dispatched: <id> <round>
<action> <time> <session>`; the builder is told its id and passes it (`--dispatch <id>`, or the
same variable) to `note`, `ask`, `built`, `integrate --aborted` and `import --done`: a result is
accepted under the lock for its own open dispatch only — a builder taken for dead that finishes
late is told "your dispatch was superseded: stop", and closes nothing. Whenever a builder's Agent call
ends — returned, failed, never started — the conductor asks `mm dispatch --status <id>`; still
open (a `note` alone is no result), it closes it: `mm dispatch --close <id> "<why>"`. A slice is **in flight** from its `Dispatched:` until that id
is closed — `completed` by `Built:` for the round, `blocked` by a `## Question`, `aborted`,
`failed` or `not started` by `--close` — and nothing but `building` is said of it meanwhile: not
`rework`, not `resume`, not `integrate`, so no second builder enters its worktree while the
first is at work. An open dispatch from **another session** is *foreign*: younger than `stale:`
minutes (default 20), or with a sign of life, it is a `NEEDS-HUMAN` — a second conductor, a
`/clear` minutes after a dispatch, a resumed conversation whose builders may be back; the human
closes it or waits — never a silent double dispatch. Past `stale:` with no sign of life, foreign
or own, the dispatch is the **backstop's**: taken as dead, closed as such, the action said
again, told it resumes, and a second builder finds the work on disk. A sign of life is a commit
on the branch, a non-ignored path changed in the worktree, a path the gate's `DEBRIS` names
written there (a suite's artifacts) or a `note`: an ignored data directory `setup:` keeps alive
is none. An `## Answer` newer than the last `Dispatched:` is `resume` at once. The conductor
adds its own veto inside a conversation — never a dispatch for a slice whose builder is in its
task list — and ends with `/clear` only when no card carries its session's id open; past the
conversation's third `land` it starts nothing new. Verified at build time, not assumed: that
`session_id` is stable across `/compact` and changes at `/clear`, and whether a resumed
conversation brings its background agents back — one log line each; the plan's rules hold either
way, the foreign-and-young case going to the human.

## `mm land <slice>`
Under the lock, on a slice not in flight, built this round, with an applicable PASS (its `TREE:`
the tip's, its `INPUTS:` the manifest's) and the tip integrated; in phases, each recognized as
done *before* the ordinary preconditions are applied, so a cut anywhere is finished by the next
`land`. **Nothing commits on the integration between phase 2 and phase 4**: the candidate is a
ref, never a header. Before phase 2, `refs/mismagent/landing/<stem>` records the branch's tip as
it was and the integration's: a landing ref is recognized before any ordinary predicate (a
rebased tip is no longer `Tip:`, and "built this round" must not stop its own land), and `next`
says `land` for it first.
1. the checkout's review files, notes and proposals published as `review: <stem>` — no code
   changes, every certificate stands;
2. the integration's code tree still the one recorded at `integrate`, else refused: `integrate`
   again (outside the lock); the branch rebased over the commits since — card commits and the
   review commit, the two union files at most — in the worktree, which must be clean (a modified
   tracked file names the path); a cut mid-rebase with no unmerged file is continued here;
3. on the rebased tip, one sha read under the lock: its `TREE:` the reviewed and the gated one,
   the manifest the reviewed `INPUTS:` (else "review again", what moved named);
   `<integration>..<tip>` holds no merge and no commit breaking the `.mismagent/` rule; `check
   --base` passes from the merge-base; `check` for the slice afresh, its card from the tip's
   tree; the checkout clean on every path the branch touches (an untracked file of that name:
   `wait`, naming it); then `refs/mismagent/candidates/<stem>` set to that sha in the common dir;
4. `merge --ff-only <the candidate ref's sha>` in the checkout — never a branch name;
5. doing → done with `Landed: <sha>`, commit; the candidate ref deleted;
6. `git worktree remove` (not forced: ignored files are disposable by definition — the shots were
   read at review, `setup:`'s output is the project's — a tracked or untracked file left there
   refuses) and `branch -d`, each skipped when already gone; refused: `Cleanup: pending` on the
   done card and a `FOR-HUMAN` line, never an action again — `mm land <done slice>` by hand
   retries it.

Recognition at the next `land`: a candidate ref whose sha is the branch's tip and descends from
the integration's tip is past phase 3 — phase 4; one already an ancestor of the integration is
past phase 4 — phase 5; any other is discarded and phase 2 runs again (the integration moved
since). A done card with a `Worktree:` is at phase 6.

## `mm next` with N builders
Eligibility first, then order. A slice in flight is `building`, nothing else; a slice with a
`## Question` unanswered is `blocked`, nothing else — the conductor settles it in that tick or
forwards it (`mm wait <slice> human`, which `mm answer` clears) and the others go on; a slice
waiting for the human, or with an aborted `integrate` past its `escalate`, is eligible for
nothing and named by `status`. Otherwise, in this order each tick, one action, equals by number:
`start <slice>` for a doing slice whose provisioning is incomplete; `land` a slice with an
applicable PASS this round at its tip and the tip integrated; `integrate` one built this round
whose merge-base's code tree is behind the integration's (never one whose last `integrate` was
aborted: that one is `escalate`), or whose rebase is open; **`rework`** one whose latest review
this round is REWORK — unresolved until the next `built`, whatever moved: its findings stand,
and this predicate comes before `review`'s; `review` one built this round, integrated, with no
applicable review this round (a PASS whose `TREE:` or `INPUTS:` moved is no review: the slice is
reviewed again without another build); `escalate`, `stuck` and DIRECT as today; `resume` one
with an answer, a dead dispatch or a tip past `Built:`; then `start`, which takes the next todo
slice whose `After:` names done slices only — `After:` is the declared prerequisite, a cycle is a
`check` error, no `After:` is the author's word that nothing comes before — if a slot is free
(doing slices < `parallel`); a `model` or `refactor` slice runs alone: when it is next in order
nothing else starts, and it starts when nothing is doing; the design pass, `catalog` and
`conventions` wait for the doing slices to land. The conductor dispatches the builder in the
background and comes back; `building` is a wait of a few minutes, with the worktree's age; a
notification wakes the conductor early, the card is the record.

## What should move
Paired runs, `parallel: 1` and `2`, same cards, same starting commit, same models; outside the
bench's fresh-session mode (it disables background tasks: a background builder cannot exist
there — `parallel: 2` under the bench is sequential).
- Wall-clock per release, human waits taken out: the gain; the DAG's width reported beside it, so
  a serial release is not read as a scheduler's failure.
- `integrate` rounds per land, code conflicts apart from the union files': the price.
- Reviews redone, by cause as written in the refusal — a landed slice under it, `README.md` or a
  mode bit, the slice's own inputs: whether a narrower code tree is worth it.
- Suite runs per slice: at `built`, at the reviewer's gate, and at `integrate` or `land` only when
  the code tree moved; `check` at each; every run writes `Gate:`, so `mm log` sees them all.
- Dispatches closed by the conductor, foreign ones sent to the human, and backstops: how often a
  builder is cut, and how long it took to see.

## Not done
- Slices that need each other's code mid-build are not parallel, by `After:`; no attempt to
  predict from a slice's text that two independent-looking slices touch one file — the second's
  rebase conflicts, or its review is redone on the landed code, and `integrate` resolves it.
- A database or a service shared by two gates (two suites on one Postgres): `setup:` gives each
  worktree its own, or the project stays at `parallel: 1`. Dependencies per worktree
  (`node_modules`, a venv) are paid per slice by `setup:`; a project where that costs minutes
  shares a cache its own way, or stays at 1. What a cut `setup:` left outside the worktree is the
  project's to make idempotent.
- A builder alive and silent for `stale:` minutes — a suite longer than that writing nothing
  the gate's `DEBRIS` names, and no `note` — meets a second builder in its worktree: `resume`
  semantics, the work on disk kept, its late result refused by its dispatch id; the threshold is
  the project's. `mm` cannot observe a Claude agent's death; the conductor can, and closes.
- A line a builder adds to `design-notes.md` or `conventions-proposals.md` after its review lands
  unreviewed: additions only, to two files of observations — a risk line holds a release, a
  proposal stops for the human — never code, never spec.
- A hand edit of a doing card in an editor while `mm note` runs: the active card is written
  through `mm` (`note`, `ask`, `answer`); a hand edit there is 0.7.27's "two hands in one file in
  the same minute", seen by the journal's heal as `NEEDS-HUMAN`, not guarded.
- Several conductors: one, in one checkout, stays; a second shows as foreign dispatches sent to
  the human, not as a refusal at its door.
- The spec branch and the store outside git: above.
